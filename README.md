# 🎬 AI Video Detector — Proof of Concept

A tool that analyses the noise signature of a video to estimate whether it was generated by an AI model (Sora, Veo) or captured on a real camera. **YouTube URL support is temporarily disabled** due to platform authentication restrictions — video file upload works great! 🎉

> ⚠️ **Proof of Concept:** The classification thresholds are exploratory and derived from a small sample of known videos. More data is needed to fine-tune them. Treat results as an indicator, not a verdict.

---

## 🧠 How it works

### Diffusion models are built on noise

Modern AI video generators — Sora, Veo, Runway, Kling — are all based on **diffusion models**. The core idea: start with pure random Gaussian noise, then iteratively denoise it, step by step, until a coherent image or video frame emerges. The model learns to reverse a noise-addition process, guided by a text prompt or conditioning signal.

This means every frame of an AI-generated video begins its life as noise. Even after the denoising process completes, the model leaves behind a characteristic residual — a noise fingerprint that differs from the kind of noise found in real camera footage.

### 📷 Real camera noise vs. AI noise

A real camera introduces noise through physical processes: photon shot noise, sensor read noise, thermal noise, and JPEG/H.264 compression artifacts. This noise is spatially incoherent and statistically predictable — it sits in a specific range.

AI-generated video has a fundamentally different noise profile depending on the model:

- **Sora** aggressively denoises its output, producing frames that are *too clean* — the high-frequency noise is suppressed well below what a real camera would produce.
- **Veo** retains stronger diffusion noise, leaving elevated high-frequency residuals that sit *above* the real-camera range.
- **Real footage** falls in between — neither too clean nor too noisy.

### 🔬 Laplacian high-pass filtering

To extract the noise signature from a video frame, we apply a **Laplacian filter** — a second-order derivative operator that responds strongly to rapid intensity changes (edges and noise) while ignoring smooth regions.

For each sampled frame:

1. Convert to grayscale
2. Apply the Laplacian kernel (`cv2.Laplacian`)
3. Take the absolute value of the response
4. Compute the **mean** and **standard deviation** across all pixels

These two numbers — `noise_mean` and `noise_std` — characterise the intensity and spread of the high-frequency content. Averaging across 15 frames spread through the video smooths out any single-frame anomalies.

### 📊 Classification thresholds

The thresholds below were derived empirically by running the analysis on known Sora, Veo, and real-camera videos. They are a starting point — **not a trained classifier**:

| Classification | Condition |
|---|---|
| 🤖 AI-Generated (Sora-style) | `noise_std < 4.5` AND `noise_mean < 2.0` |
| 🤖 AI-Generated (Veo-style) | `noise_std > 6.5` AND `noise_mean > 3.0` |
| 🎥 Likely Real | everything in between |

**Observed ranges so far:**
- Sora: `noise_std` 1.97–3.94, `noise_mean` ~1.5–1.9
- Veo: `noise_std` 8.28–16.85, `noise_mean` ~4.0–8.0
- Real: `noise_std` 4.27–5.13, `noise_mean` ~2.5–2.8

## 🛠️ Stack

- **Backend:** FastAPI + yt-dlp + OpenCV + NumPy
- **Frontend:** Vanilla HTML/CSS/JS (no dependencies)
- **Hosting:** Hugging Face Spaces (Docker)

---

## ⚠️ Limitations

- Thresholds are heuristic, not trained on labelled data — more samples needed 📈
- Compression, re-encoding, or post-processing can shift noise numbers
- YouTube URL download is currently unreliable due to platform-side auth blocks — use file upload instead
- Works best on uncompressed or lightly compressed MP4s
